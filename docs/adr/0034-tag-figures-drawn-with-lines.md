# Tag figures drawn with lines

ADR-0027 tagged only embedded images, and sent every picture drawn with lines to a page crop. A test saved from a browser as a PDF keeps each SVG graph or structure as the paths it drew, and a chemistry program does the same with its structures, so the PDF has no image for them, and many of their figures went untagged. A teacher reads a missing tag as a fault, and each one meant a crop by hand.

A PDF does not say where such a figure is, so Test Parrot finds it from the ink. Paths that overlap or nearly touch are one drawing, including a path that only clips, since a browser clips each SVG to its own frame and that frame holds its labels; ink within a drawing's reach, such as a graph's points between its axes, is part of it too. A box reaches only its sides: a question's frame does not gather everything inside it. Short words that touch a drawing are part of it before anything is judged, so an atom label set between two bonds joins them into one structure; a line of the question is not a label. Words include atom labels a drawing program paints as stencil masks rather than text, and letters of a font saved as outlines, which are small filled shapes; a dot standing alone stays drawing.

A drawing is a figure when it has at least ten curves, like a graph, or at least two slanted lines, like the smallest structure of two bonds and a label, or when its level and plumb lines rule the cells of a table, dashed or solid, or divide a box, as an area model's lines and shaded cell divide its square. Rules, underline blanks and empty answer boxes, rounded or dashed, are none of these, and are never tagged however much text sits in them; nor is a row of radio buttons, check boxes or ovals to fill in, which are small closed shapes with nothing slanted. Level and plumb lines join only where they meet, so tables set one under another as answer choices stay apart. A figure's strokes must reach past an icon's size and be longer than a letter's, so a drawn asterisk, a menu's arrow or a triangle sign in a line of text is not one, and the figure must be at least 25 by 10 thousandths of the page with its labels, smaller than an image needs, since a labelled structure measured about 55 by 14; a figure of filled shapes only is held to an image's size. Drawings on one line joined by a reaction arrow, including an equilibrium's half-arrows, or by a plus sign between two drawings, are one figure, typed structures included; drawings beside each other with anything else between them, such as “vs.” or a choice's letter, stay apart, because an assistant can set two tags side by side but cannot split one. A figure gets an Image Tag in reading order with the images on its page, and its labeled copy is marked the same way.

The picture a drawn figure's tag names is its region of the page rendered at print resolution, not bytes from the original, since there are none. That relaxes ADR-0027's rule that a tag's picture is the document's own image; it is still a pure function of the PDF's bytes, so the same PDF gets the same tags.

The rule is a heuristic, and it errs toward tagging: an extra tag costs the assistant a line in its report, while a missing one costs the teacher a crop. A tag may hold a table, typed text, or part of a larger figure, and the assistant's instructions say to transcribe it, combine it with its neighbours, or leave it out. A figure of only level and plumb lines that is not a table, such as a bar chart, is not tagged and is still cropped, and a scatter of points that touch each other may be read as text.
