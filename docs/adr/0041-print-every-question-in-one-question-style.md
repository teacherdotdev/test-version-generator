# Print every question in one Question Style

Teachers coming from other tools expect their tests to look the way those tools print them: an answer blank before each number, as test generators print, or as many questions to a page as will fit. Since ADR-0029 Test Parrot printed one look only: a Multiple Choice letter circled on its answer, T and F circled beside a True/False number, and no blanks before numbers.

An Exam now has one **Question Style**, chosen from the Format menu's Questions submenu, which shows the current style and offers three, each with a line saying what it does and a small picture of the one thing it changes most, with a "1." where it prints the question's number. It is presentation like the heading and text sizes (ADR-0025): stored on the Exam, saved and undone with it, and reflowing the sheet as soon as it changes. A style per question or per Question Type was considered and left out until a teacher asks: the request was for a test that looks like the ones they know, and a per-Exam setting can gain finer ones later without changing what an Exam stores.

| Style | Before a number | Answers | Word Bank | Short Answer with no Work Space | Spacing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | T and F to circle on True/False; nothing on Multiple Choice | `A.`, in the question's columns | beside its Items where it fits, above otherwise | no room | the sheet's own |
| Classic | an answer blank on Multiple Choice and True/False | `a.`, in the question's columns | lettered `a.`, above its Items | three ruled lines | the sheet's own |
| Condensed | as Standard | `A.`, across the line where they fit | as Standard, beside more often | three ruled lines, closer together | closer together, and every Work Space's rows too |

- **Standard** is the sheet as it printed before, and the default; an Exam that never chose a style stores nothing and prints exactly as it did.
- **Classic** is modeled on common test-generator output: an answer space before each objective question's number ("____ 1."), space below open-ended questions with answer lines, True/False printed without answer choices unless asked for, and a matching group's choices lettered a–z and listed after its instructions, above its numbered questions. Such tools differ in how many answer lines they rule and which letter style they default to; three lines and "a." are our reading of typical printed samples.
- **Condensed** saves paper: nothing added before a number, questions closer together, and Multiple Choice answers laid across the line in four, or else two, columns wherever every answer fits one line of a column. How wide an answer is, is a measurement, so it is decided with the pagination, by the same injected `Measure`; an answer holding a picture or a table keeps its columns. It saves paper without taking away room to write: every Work Space keeps its rows, set closer together (below).

A fourth style, Worksheet — a long blank to write "true" or "false" on and the Word Bank always beside its Items — was built and then withdrawn before it shipped: it differed from Classic in too little to be worth a choice, and a Word Bank's place is now decided by whether it fits, or by the teacher for one question. An Exam or a plan that names a style this build does not know prints as Standard.

Every Matching Item keeps its blank under every style, Condensed included: it is where a Matching answer is written, not an addition. A Multiple Choice Part keeps its capital letters under every style, so they never read as the lower-case Part letters beside them. A style never lays answers out in fewer columns than the teacher set; Classic prints "stacked or in the question's columns" by keeping the teacher's own setting, which is one column unless they chose more.

The style is resolved where every other content decision is, in the Export Document (ADR-0001): the planned question carries what prints before its number, the grid and Word Bank carry the letters as the test prints them, and the work space is the one the style resolves. So print, PDF, DOCX, the Export Preview and the sheet draw the same thing without each knowing the styles, and packing measures what they draw. The only thing an adapter reads from the style is how far apart questions stand, which the Layout Plan names beside its text size. The answer blank is the same seven underscores, in the same wider number column, that an Export Record from before Sections printed, so those records reprint through the same path.

The Answer Key does not change with the style. Its letters stay capitals, as a test generator's own key reads "ANS: A" beside a test lettered "a."; the question keeps its capitals for the key while its grid prints the style's letters.

## Work Space

The style supplies the room a Short Answer question or Short Answer Part leaves only where the teacher has set none. A Work Space the teacher set always wins, "None" included. Choosing None used to delete the position's setting, since no room and no setting printed alike; under a style that rules lines that is no longer true, so None is then stored as a zero-height Work Space, and it keeps meaning none whatever style the Exam later takes. Switching style never touches a stored Work Space. The sheet's Work Space menu and handle start from what the position prints, so a position showing a style's lines shows Lined space checked.

Condensed once supplied no room, so switching an Exam that showed Classic's lines to Condensed took every Short Answer's lines away — the one thing a test cannot do without. It now rules Classic's three lines where the teacher set none, and makes every Work Space denser rather than smaller in count: a stored Work Space is a number of rows (its height in 32px rows, as before), and the style decides how far apart they lie on the page — a third of an inch under Standard and Classic, a quarter (24px, college-ruled) under Condensed. The pitch is applied when the plan lays the space out, never written back, so a stored height reads the same under every style and returns to its old size when the style does. The sheet's handle drags and snaps by whole rows at the pitch the page shows.

A ruled space's first row is a quarter shorter than the rest (24px under a 32px pitch, 18px under Condensed). A full first row left more room between a stem and its first rule than between any two rules, so back to back the stem sat as far from its own lines as from the last question's and read as belonging to neither. The question's last line of text already ends below its letters; the quarter taken off is about the x-height of the body type, the part of a written line no student needs above the first rule. No padding is added below the space instead: a Work Space of N rows takes one quarter-row less than it did, the first rule sits that much closer to its stem, and the rest of the page moves up with it. Blank room is laid out in the same rows, so switching a space between blank and ruled moves nothing. The planned work space records its pitch and first row (`pitch`, `firstRow`) beside its height and its count of rules, so print, the PDF and DOCX rule exactly what packing measured; a plan recorded before either was stated draws 32px rows, as it printed.

## Where a Word Bank prints

Standard and Condensed once placed a Word Bank by its count — beside its Items up to five answers, above them past that. Few answers can still be too wide to stand beside the Items, and many short ones fit there easily, especially in small text. The fit rule measures instead: a Word Bank goes beside its Items when its widest answer, measured on one line at the Exam's text size, fits a column that still leaves the Items `MATCHING_PROMPTS_MIN_WIDTH` (240px) past their number column on a page as wide as the Exam's margins leave — 180px under Condensed, so it goes beside more often there — and otherwise above them in two columns. A bank with more than twice as many answers as there are Items, and more than five, goes above: beside, the set would stand as tall as its bank, and a set split across pages repeats its whole bank beside each piece, so a tall bank beside few Items spends the most paper for the least gain. Classic puts its bank above its Items, as the tools it is modeled on print.

**Every Matching position stores a concrete layout, Beside or Above; there is no Auto.** A first cut offered Auto, Beside items and Above items, with Auto worked out by the Layout Plan on every layout. It was withdrawn as answer columns' Auto was (`columnsOf`): a layout that changed itself when an answer was edited, or the margins moved, was one a teacher could not rely on, and a menu whose checked row said "Auto" did not say what the page showed. Now the fit rule runs once, when a position takes its layout, and the result is stored:

- **When a Matching question arrives on an Exam** — Add, Add all, a drag, or a Question supplied whole — it takes the layout its Question Style and the fit rule give it then. A Duplicate takes its original's, as it takes its answer columns and Work Space. An imported Exam Record position that carries `wordBankLayout` keeps it; one that does not is placed on import by the same rule (the import review's preview places it the same way).
- **When the Exam changes Question Style**, every Matching position takes the new style's layout again, over any the teacher flipped, in the same undoable step as the style itself: a style is a preset for how the whole sheet prints, and picking one should show what it looks like. Choosing the style the Exam already has changes nothing. A teacher may then flip any question, from its context menu on the sheet, under **Answer format**: **Word Bank** › **Beside items** or **Above items**, for the whole selection's Matching questions at once, undoable like any other edit. That choice holds until the Exam changes style again. Nothing else re-runs the rule: editing a question's answers, its text size or its margins leaves its layout as it is.
- **Measuring stays out of the store.** The store is pure and holds no DOM, but placing a bank needs `Measure.bankAnswerWidth`. The editor injects `domMeasure`'s into the store when it creates it, and into the import, and each action that places a bank calls it once and stores the result. Undo and redo restore stored state, and every Layout Plan reads only what is stored, so plans stay deterministic and no layout measures where a bank goes. Passing the layout in from the editor on each action was rejected because Add, Add all, drag, Duplicate, import and the style switch all reach the store by different routes, and each would need its own measuring; a text-length estimate in the store was rejected because a Word Bank answer may be mathematics or a picture. Without an injected measure — in tests and anywhere nothing can measure — a bank is placed by its count, beside up to five answers.
- **A position stored without a layout** — an Exam saved on this branch before every position stored one; none shipped — reads as the unmeasured rule gives it, the same on every read, until something sets it. It is not migrated.

Beside always stands beside, in the widest column the Items allow, its answers wrapping in it when even that is too narrow, rather than overflowing the page; Above always stands above. The Layout Plan measures only how wide a bank beside its Items is drawn — 240px, as before, unless its widest answer needs more — never where it goes. The choice is Exam presentation, carried by Export Records in their plans and by the Exam Record as a Matching position's `wordBankLayout`, which Test Parrot writes on every Matching position. This is the one per-question layout choice beside answer columns, made because it was asked for; everything else a style decides stays Exam-wide. The Answer Key does not change.

## Records

An Export Record keeps its Layout Plans, which carry everything the style decided, so a historical re-export reproduces it with no further change. The Exam Record carries the style as `questionStyle`, absent meaning Standard, so it travels in a Test Parrot Package. Adding a member a 0.3.0 consumer would drop is a minor version: Exam Record `0.4.0` is published beside `0.3.0`, carrying `questionStyle` and a Matching position's `wordBankLayout` alongside the Page Margins (ADR-0039). Test Parrot writes `0.4.0` and reads every earlier version as an Exam in the Standard style. Only a Work Space the teacher set is written into a position, a zero-height one included where the style would rule lines; the room a style rules is the style's, and travels as `questionStyle`.
